Data

All the data are public, from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (FRED) and the agencies that publish through it. This page lists every series the models use.

Where the data come from

Data setWhat it isUsed for
FRED-MD About 126 monthly US macro series (output, jobs, housing, orders, money, interest rates, prices, stock prices), maintained by Michael McCracken and Serena Ng at the St. Louis Fed. The general factors of every industry model.
FRED-MD vintages326 monthly snapshots of FRED-MD, August 1999 to September 2026, each as it was published. The real-time test: each forecast uses only the snapshot available at its date.
Industry series (FRED)192 monthly series for 13 industries, none of them in FRED-MD: production, capacity use, orders, shipments, sales, jobs and prices.The industry models and the economy model.
GDP nowcast series (FRED)35 monthly and quarterly series from the New York Fed's Staff Nowcast 2.0 list. Four Dallas and Philadelphia Fed survey indexes stand in for the ISM surveys, which are not on FRED. The GDP nowcast.
ALFRED The St. Louis Fed's archive of every past release of a FRED series. Real-time versions of the industry and GDP nowcast series. Where a series has no archive for a date, it is left out (GDP nowcast) or cut off at that date (industry models, labeled "pseudo-real-time").
BEA GDP estimatesThe advance (first), third and latest estimates of real GDP growth, from ALFRED. The "truth" each GDP nowcast is scored against.
New York Fed Nowcasting ReportsThe nowcasts the New York Fed published weekly, 2016–2019. A head-to-head comparison with our replica.

This site shows model results and series descriptions only. For the data values themselves, follow the links to FRED.

Every series

For economistsTransformations

Industry models follow FRED-MD's transformation codes: "growth rate" is the change in the log, "change in growth rate" the second difference of the log (FRED-MD's default for prices), "change" the first difference. Every series is then standardized. The GDP nowcast follows the New York Fed's Table 1: monthly % changes or changes, survey indexes in levels, quarterly series as % changes at an annual rate.
