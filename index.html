Nowcasting the US Economy

This project builds statistical models that track the US economy from the stream of official data releases: jobs, factory output, retail sales, surveys, prices and more. They estimate how fast the economy is growing right now, before the official GDP figure is published, and forecast monthly indicators for the whole economy and for 13 industries.

The government's first estimate of GDP growth for a quarter comes out about a month after the quarter ends. In the meantime, dozens of monthly statistics are published, each telling us a little about how the quarter is going. A nowcast combines them into a running estimate of the quarter's growth, updated every week as new numbers arrive. Each dot above is one week's nowcast: early on the model knows little and stays close to normal growth; as reports come in, it moves toward what the data say.

How close does it get?

We replayed every quarter from 2006 to 2019, each week using only the data published by then. On the day before the government's first estimate, the nowcast's mean squared error was 1.37, about the same as the New York Fed reports for its own model (1.3) and 65% lower than a simple benchmark that only extrapolates past GDP growth (3.93).

How to read this site

Each page starts with a plain-language explanation and a few charts. Panels marked For economists open up the equations, estimation details and full result tables.

  • GDP nowcast: every quarter's weekly nowcast against the official figures, what moved each one, and how accurate it is.
  • Industry models: forecast accuracy for national indicators and for each industry.
  • Model structure: the hidden "factors" each model uses and which data series they explain.
  • Data: a searchable list of every data series, with links to the source.
  • Methods: how the models are tested, what the scores mean, related work and caveats.
