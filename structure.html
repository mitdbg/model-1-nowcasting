Model structure

Both models explain many data series with a few hidden factors. This page shows what each factor stands for and which series it drives.

Think of a factor as a hidden index, like an unpublished "state of the economy" number. Each data series is modeled as a mix of a few factors plus noise of its own. A series' loading on a factor is how strongly it moves with that factor. The models only allow the loadings that make economic sense: a jobs series can load on the labor factor, a steel series on the steel factor, and so on.

The GDP nowcast model has 35 series and six hidden components:

  • Trend: the economy's long-run growth rate, which drifts slowly. Only GDP and GDI load on it.
  • Global: the overall pace of real activity. Almost every series loads on it.
  • Soft: business sentiment, from regional Fed manufacturing and services surveys.
  • Nominal: prices and series measured in dollars.
  • Labor: the job market.
  • COVID: a factor that exists only from March to September 2020, to absorb the pandemic's extreme swings.

Bars show the estimated loading of each series on each factor (posterior mean, standardized data). Blue bars move with the factor, red bars against it. An empty cell means the model does not allow that loading.

The economy model has 243 monthly series: 126 national series from FRED-MD and 117 activity series from 13 industries. It has 17 factors: 4 economy-wide ones and one for each industry. The economy-wide factors are statistical constructs; their names come from the series each one explains best.
