Models

Every model on this site has a name. The models in production publish forecasts, and each has its own page with an overview, its latest forecasts and its accuracy.

Names read FAMILY-VERSION-MODIFIER: GDP for the GDP nowcast, IND for the industry models; P5 marks the price version of an industry model, which forecasts prices as plain growth rates.

Reference models

These models are not published as forecasts. They are the baselines and building blocks the production models are measured against; their accuracy is on the Industry models page.

IND-G · general model

The 126 national monthly series of FRED-MD and 4 economy-wide factors. Every industry model is this model plus one industry's series and two industry factors, so IND-G is the baseline for the question "does information about an industry help?" Accuracy on national indicators →

IND-ECON · economy model

All 13 industries at once: 243 monthly series (126 national series and 117 industry activity series) and 17 factors, 4 economy-wide and one for each industry. Its forecasts of industry activity series are compared with each industry model's on that model's page, and it is tested on national indicators on the Industry models page. How the models work: factors

Every model here explains many data series with a few hidden factors. Think of a factor as a hidden index, like an unpublished "state of the economy" number. Each data series is modeled as a mix of a few factors plus noise of its own. A series' loading on a factor is how strongly it moves with that factor, and the models only allow the loadings that make economic sense: a jobs series can load on the labor factor, a steel series on the steel factor. Which series drive which factor is shown on each model's page (GDP-2, for example) and, for the economy model IND-ECON, below.
